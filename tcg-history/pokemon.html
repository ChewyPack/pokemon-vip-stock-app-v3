The story behind the cards

Pokémon Trading Card Game History

From its beginnings in Japan to a global hobby built around collecting, trading, and competitive play, the Pokémon TCG has kept generations of fans opening packs and chasing favorite cards.

1996

The Japanese debut

The Pokémon Trading Card Game launched in Japan in October 1996, bringing the world of Pokémon into a collectible tabletop card game. Early cards and sets soon became the foundation of a hobby that would grow alongside the video games and animated series.

1998–99

The game reaches more players

The English-language game arrived in the United States in 1999. The original Base Set introduced many collectors to familiar cards such as Charizard, Blastoise, and Venusaur, and helped make opening booster packs a lasting part of Pokémon fandom.

2000s

New regions, new eras

As new Pokémon generations arrived, the card game expanded through new sets, mechanics, artwork styles, and tournament formats. Older cards also became an important part of collecting, with condition, print runs, and set identity shaping how collectors valued them.

2010s

Collecting becomes more connected

Online communities, card databases, marketplace tools, and organized play made it easier to compare collections, learn set histories, trade, and follow prices. Full-art cards and special rarities brought new attention to illustration and card design.

2020s

A global collecting community

Modern expansions combine new gameplay mechanics with alternate illustrations, premium collections, and nostalgia-driven releases. The hobby now spans players, set collectors, sealed-product collectors, artists, local shops, and communities sharing sightings and discoveries.

2026

Thirty years of Pokémon TCG

The 30th Celebration release marks three decades since the game's Japanese debut, giving collectors a chance to celebrate the cards, characters, and memories that have shaped the hobby.
